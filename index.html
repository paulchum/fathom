02 / FIELD NOTES

A little context.
A better question.

Three ideas worth keeping in mind
before turning a number into a decision.

NOTE / 001
1.0 → 1.4

PUE is a ratio.
Not the whole story.

A lower PUE reduces overhead for the same IT energy. It doesn’t tell you how useful the computation is, or how the electricity was generated.

NOTE / 002
Q = ṁ cₚ ΔT

Warmer return.
Less circulating flow.

For the same heat and fluid, doubling the temperature rise halves the required flow. Equipment limits still decide which temperatures are possible.

NOTE / 003
MW × HOURS = MWh

Power is a rate.
Energy is the total.

One megawatt running for one hour uses one megawatt-hour. Your bill follows energy, the tariff and other charges—not nameplate capacity alone.

01 Power & energy +
Facility power = IT power × PUE
Annual energy = facility power × hours
Energy cost = MWh × 1,000 × price/kWh

Use average IT load and a consistent, energy-weighted PUE across the selected hours. The comparison holds IT load, hours and price fixed. Currency changes the label, not the exchange rate.

Source: US Department of Energy ↗
02 Cooling & circulation +
Heat = IT power × liquid capture fraction
Mass flow = heat / (specific heat × ΔT)
Pump input = pressure drop × volume flow / efficiency

Assumes a single-phase fluid and constant properties. The water defaults (4.18 kJ/kg·K and 1,000 kg/m³) are screening approximations. Specify your actual properties. Flow represents circulation, not water consumption. Facility overhead and pump heat feedback are excluded from the heat load.

Source: DOE heat-transfer handbook ↗
Fluid-property reference: NIST ↗
03 Your scenarios & privacy +

Calculations run in your browser. Saved comparisons stay in this browser’s local storage; clear them any time. Share links contain your input values in the URL fragment. Anyone with the link can read them. The app has no analytics, account system or application backend. The hosting provider may retain ordinary access logs.

JSON exports include inputs, results, units, assumptions and sources. CSV exports include inputs and results for spreadsheets.

04 Scope & source code +

This is a new public workbench informed by FATHOM’s earlier infrastructure modelling. It estimates energy and flow; it does not certify designs, site feasibility, water availability or equipment performance.

The source is public on GitHub. No project licence has been granted at this time.
